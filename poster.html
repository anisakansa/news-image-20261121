The Hague — Landmark verdict

MYANMAR COMMITTED GENOCIDE, WORLD COURT RULES

The International Court of Justice finds the state responsible for the 2017 atrocities against the Rohingya — the first time it has ever held a state guilty of genocide — and orders return, restitution and compensation.
By nine votes to six, judges in The Hague held that Myanmar’s military killed thousands, committed widespread sexual violence and razed villages in Rakhine State, and that the only reasonable inference was an intent to destroy the Rohingya. The court ordered investigations and prosecutions, the safe return of the displaced, and compensation to be fixed later. Myanmar called the ruling “a great disappointment”. The judgment is final and binding — but the court has no force to enforce it.

Poland — NATO’s flank

Russian missiles fall on Poland

Two suspected Russian missiles and a jet-powered drone came down in south-east Poland during a huge overnight strike on Ukraine.

NATO chief Mark Rutte called it a “grave violation” of allied airspace. No one was hurt. PM Donald Tusk called it a likely provocation as allied drills went on nearby — and Warsaw will ask NATO for more air defences.

Brussels — The reset

Britain and EU seal £17bn deal

PM Andy Burnham hailed a “win-win” as Britain and the EU agreed to scrap most food-export checks, link carbon markets and open a youth mobility scheme for 100,000 young people for up to three years.

The package could add £17bn to the UK economy — but the deals hinge on a row over tuition fees for EU students.
